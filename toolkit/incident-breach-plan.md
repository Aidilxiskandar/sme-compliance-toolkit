## 1. Anyone who notices something: report it now
Tell the person in charge **immediately**: locked or renamed files, ransom messages, strange pop-ups, password resets you didn't ask for, money or messages sent in our name, a lost phone or laptop, or data sent to the wrong person. **The 72-hour clock for telling JPDP starts when the business finds out**, so don't wait until you're sure.

## 2. Contain it (first hour)
- [ ] Disconnect affected computers from the network (unplug the cable / turn off Wi-Fi). **Don't switch them off or wipe them**, because evidence matters.
- [ ] Change passwords and sign out all sessions on affected accounts, starting with: {{keyAccounts}}
- [ ] Remove access for anyone who shouldn't have it, including vendors' remote access.
- [ ] If money is involved: call your bank, and the National Scam Response Centre on **997**, straight away.
- [ ] Write down what you see and when (screenshots, times). This feeds the breach register.

## 3. Was personal data involved? Decide on notifying (within 72 hours)
Personal data we hold, and what a breach would mean:

{{dataTable}}

- **Tell JPDP within 72 hours** if the breach could cause significant harm (sensitive, financial or ID data, or a risk of loss, fraud or misuse) **or** affects more than 1,000 people. Use the form at pdp.gov.my or email dbnpdp@pdp.gov.my. You can send missing details within 30 days. It only counts once JPDP sends a confirmation notice.
- **Tell the affected people within 7 days** of telling JPDP if it could cause them significant harm: what happened, what data, what we're doing, and what they should do.
- If we miss the 72 hours, we still notify, and explain the delay in writing with evidence.
- Processors (vendors, freelancers) must tell us at once about breaches on their side.

## 4. Get help and report
- IT support / vendor: {{itSupport}}
- **Cyber999** (CyberSecurity Malaysia) for reporting and advice on cyber incidents: see mycert.org.my for the current reporting channels.
- **Police** report for crimes such as fraud, extortion or theft.
- The platform's own support for hacked social media, marketplace or email accounts.

## 5. Recover
- [ ] Restore from a backup taken **before** the incident, and check it is complete and clean.
- [ ] Bring systems back one by one, and watch for the problem coming back.
- [ ] Tell staff (and customers, if affected) what they need to know.

## 6. Record and learn
- [ ] Add it to the **breach register**, even if we didn't need to notify, and keep it for at least 2 years.
- [ ] Within two weeks: short review of what happened, what worked and what to change. Update the risk register and policies.

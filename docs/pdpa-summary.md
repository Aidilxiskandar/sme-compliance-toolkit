# PDPA summary for small businesses

> **Not legal advice.** This is a plain-language summary to help a small business find its duties. It was checked against official sources on 11 Nov 2026 (listed at the end). Laws and guidelines change, so check the [JPDP website](https://www.pdp.gov.my) before relying on any figure.

The Personal Data Protection Act 2010 [Act 709] applies to anyone who processes personal data in **commercial transactions** in Malaysia. A cafe that keeps customer phone numbers is covered just like a bank. Since the 2024 amendments, the business deciding why and how data is used is called the **data controller** (previously "data user"). A business that processes data on someone else's behalf is a **data processor**.

## 1. The seven principles (the core duties)
| Principle | What it means for a small business |
|---|---|
| General | Only process personal data with the person's consent, unless an exception applies (for example, to fulfil a contract with them). Sensitive data needs **explicit** consent. |
| Notice and Choice | Tell people in writing, in Malay and English, what you collect, why, who you share it with and how to contact you. |
| Disclosure | Don't use or share the data for a purpose they weren't told about. |
| Security | Take practical steps to protect the data from loss, misuse and unauthorised access. **Since 1 April 2025 this also binds data processors directly.** |
| Retention | Don't keep data longer than the purpose needs. Destroy or anonymise it after. |
| Data Integrity | Keep the data accurate, complete and up to date. |
| Access | Let people see and correct the data you hold about them. |

**Sensitive personal data** means data about physical or mental health, political opinions, religious or similar beliefs, the commission or alleged commission of an offence, and (since the 2024 amendments) **biometric data**. It needs explicit consent and gets stricter treatment. For example, any breach involving it counts as "significant harm" (section 3).

## 2. What changed with the 2024 amendments
The Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Act 2024 [Act A1727] came into force in three stages, set by gazette **P.U.(B) 522** (24 Dec 2024):

| Date | What came in (by topic) |
|---|---|
| 1 Jan 2025 | Sections 7, 11, 13, 14 of the amending Act: smaller items, such as serving notices electronically |
| 1 Apr 2025 | Sections 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10, 12: "data user" renamed "data controller"; biometric data added to sensitive data; **processors bound by the Security Principle**; maximum fine for breaching the principles raised to **RM1 million and/or 3 years**; cross-border transfer rules changed |
| 1 Jun 2025 | Sections 6 and 9: **mandatory Data Protection Officer (DPO)** and **mandatory data breach notification**; also the right to data portability |

The gazette lists section numbers only. The topic for each date comes from the guidelines and law-firm summaries in the sources.

## 3. Data breach notification (since 1 June 2025)
Source: JPDP *Guideline on Data Breach Notification*, v1.0, 25 Feb 2025.

**Who:** every data controller. Processors do not notify the Commissioner directly, but the controller **must put in its contract** that the processor tells it promptly about any breach and helps it respond (para 12).

**When you must notify:**
| Notify | When | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| The Commissioner | The breach causes or is likely to cause **significant harm**, *or* affects **more than 1,000 people** ("significant scale") | As soon as practicable, **no later than 72 hours** (see "when the clock starts") |
| The affected people | The breach causes or is likely to cause **significant harm** (the 1,000-person scale test does *not* apply here) | Without unnecessary delay, **no later than 7 days** after notifying the Commissioner |

**"Significant harm"** means a risk that the data could lead to physical harm, financial loss, a hit to credit records, or loss of or damage to property; could be misused for illegal purposes; **is sensitive personal data**; or, combined with other information, could enable **identity fraud** (para 5.2).

**When the 72-hour clock starts:** the guideline says "from the occurrence of the breach", but its worked examples start the clock when the controller **is told, realises, or confirms** the breach (para 6.2). For example, it starts when a lost USB stick is reported, when you realise an email went to the wrong person, or when you confirm that a system was compromised. A **ransomware** attack starts it once you confirm personal data was affected. If a processor has the breach, the clock starts when it tells you or you find clear evidence, whichever is earlier. The safe reading: treat the clock as running from the moment anyone reports a possible incident.

**How:** the online form on [pdp.gov.my](https://www.pdp.gov.my), or the Annex B form by email to `dbnpdp@pdp.gov.my` or by hard copy. The notification only counts once the Commissioner sends a **confirmation notice**. Missing details can follow in phases within **30 days**. If you miss the 72 hours, send a written explanation with evidence.

**Records:** keep a **breach register** for **at least 2 years**, including breaches that did *not* need to be notified. It must record when you became aware, the root cause, data types, number of people and records affected, likely consequences, a timeline, containment steps, and what was notified (or why not) (para 14).

**A plan is required:** controllers must have a breach management and response plan covering identification and escalation, roles, containment, the decision to notify, communications and a post-incident review, plus periodic training and drills (para 11). This is what toolkit item 5 is for.

**Penalty for failing to notify the Commissioner:** a fine up to **RM250,000** and/or up to **2 years** in prison (section 12B(3), quoted in para 2.2).

## 4. Data Protection Officer (since 1 June 2025)
Source: JPDP *Guideline on Appointment of Data Protection Officer*, v1.0, 25 Feb 2025.

Both controllers **and processors** must appoint a DPO if they process (para 4.2):
- personal data of **more than 20,000** people, or
- **sensitive personal data, including financial information**, of **more than 10,000** people, or
- data in activities needing **regular and systematic monitoring**. Examples given: tracking and profiling people for **behavioural advertising**, recommendation algorithms on a retail site, health wearables, and activities involving **CCTV** or connected devices, which the guideline says "may constitute" monitoring. A loyalty programme that only manages accounts and doesn't track buying behaviour may not count.

The rules for the DPO:
- **Who:** an existing employee, or an outsourced person or firm. An outsourced firm must name a person-in-charge, and a contract of at least 2 years is recommended.
- **Requirements:** resident in Malaysia (180+ days a year) **or** easily contactable, and proficient in **both Malay and English**.
- **Registration:** register the DPO's business contact details with the Commissioner through the SPDP portal within **21 days** of appointing them, and update any change within **14 days**.
- **If you decide you don't need one:** you *may* keep a record of why (para 4.5). The toolkit recommends always doing this.

> **Grey area for small shops: CCTV.** Almost every small business has CCTV, and the guideline lists CCTV as something that "may constitute" systematic monitoring. It does not say a shop's security cameras automatically trigger a DPO. Until JPDP clarifies, the toolkit's advice is to **write down the reasoning** (what the cameras cover, who views them, how long footage is kept) and review it once a year.

## 5. Registration as a data controller
Source: *Commissioner's Circular No. 1/2026, Registration of Data Controllers* (in force 1 Jun 2026); JPDP list of the 13 classes (from P.U.(A) 336/2013 and P.U.(A) 326/2016).

A business must **register** with the Commissioner if all three are true:
1. it processes personal data in commercial transactions,
2. it falls in one of the **13 classes**, and
3. it is a sole proprietorship, partnership (but **not** an LLP/PLT), Sdn Bhd or Bhd.

The classes most relevant to small businesses: **Health** (licensed private healthcare facilities, registered private medical and dental clinics, registered pharmacy companies); **Education** (private higher education institutions, and registered private schools or private educational institutions under the Education Act 1996); **Service** (companies or partnerships in law, audit, **accounting**, engineering and architecture; **wholesale and retail**; private employment agencies); **Tourism and hospitality** (tour operators, travel agents, registered tourist accommodation); real estate developers; direct selling; pawnbrokers; moneylenders. **Food and beverage is not a class.**

| Business type | Annual fee |
|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship | RM100 |
| Partnership | RM200 |
| Sdn Bhd | RM300 |
| Bhd | RM400 |

- **How:** apply online at `daftar.pdp.gov.my` with the licence or registration from your regulator (one registration per licence, except for the Service class). Applications are reviewed in 14 working days. Certificates last 1 to 5 years.
- **Display:** show the certificate where it can be seen at your main premises.
- **Renewal:** renew within 90 days before expiry.
- **Penalties:** processing without registering carries up to **RM500,000** and/or **3 years**. Continuing after the certificate expires carries up to **RM250,000** and/or **2 years**.

## 6. What this means for the five case-study businesses
These are our assessments, not rulings. ⚠ marks a point that still needs checking against the original order or an official answer.

| | Register? | DPO? | Breach notification angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01 Kopi Senja** (cafe) | **No**: food and beverage is not a class | **Probably no** (about 800 customers, under 20,000). ⚠ The CCTV grey area applies, so record the reasoning | The 800-number list is under the 1,000 scale test, but **staff IC and bank photos** could enable identity fraud, which counts as significant harm |
| **02 Klinik Damai Sentosa** (GP clinic) | **Yes**: Health class, private medical clinic | **Yes**: sensitive health data for about 14,000 patients (over 10,000) | **Any** breach of medical records counts as significant harm, whatever its size (the guideline's own example), so notify the Commissioner *and* patients. Ransomware on the server PC is the scenario to plan for. |
| **03 Rumah Batik Lestari** (online shop) | ⚠ **Possibly**: the Service class includes "wholesale and retail", so the exact wording of P.U.(A) 326/2016 needs checking | **Likely yes**: under 20,000 customers, but the **ad pixel and retargeting audiences** are behavioural advertising, the guideline's first monitoring example | Over 1,000 customer addresses means significant scale. The freelancer counts as a **processor**, which needs contract terms. |
| **04 Tan & Rahim Associates** (accounting firm) | **Yes**: Service class, accounting (partnership: RM200 a year) | **Yes**: financial information counts with sensitive data for the 10,000 threshold, and it holds payroll for about 11,000 people | For payroll it is a **processor** for its clients and must tell them promptly about breaches. For its own client records it is a controller. Financial data counts as significant harm. |
| **05 Pusat Tuisyen Cemerlang** (tuition centre) | ⚠ **Likely yes**, if it is registered as a private educational institution under the Education Act 1996 | **Probably no** (about 850 students) | Children's medical notes are sensitive data, so a breach is significant harm. Consent for minors comes from a parent or guardian. |

## 7. Open points to verify
- ⚠ Whether online-only retailers fall under the "wholesale and retail" class (check the text of P.U.(A) 326/2016).
- ⚠ Whether tuition centres count as "private educational institutions" under the Education Act 1996 for registration.
- ⚠ Whether a small shop's security CCTV counts as "regular and systematic monitoring" for the DPO rule.
- The data portability right: only a consultation paper (No. 03/2024) was found on the JPDP site, so its practical rules are not yet covered here.
- The 1 April 2025 changes (renaming, biometric data, processor duty, RM1 million maximum fine) are from law-firm summaries matched to the gazette dates. Confirm them against the text of Act A1727.

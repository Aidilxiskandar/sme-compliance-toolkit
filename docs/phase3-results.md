# Phase 3 results: gap assessment of five fictional businesses

> Generated by `tools/evaluate.mjs` from `data/examples/`, `data/answer-keys.json` and `data/assessment.json`. Do not edit by hand. Costs are rough planning estimates (Nov 2026), not quotes.

## Method

- **Question bank:** 32 questions, one per toolkit control. Each has what to *ask* the owner and *how to check* the evidence. Levels: 0 not in place, 1 partly, 2 in place, 3 in place and checked. Score = % of level 3.
- **Three rounds per business:** (1) the owner's self-assessment, built from each profile's "What the owner says"; (2) a verified assessment of the true state, built from "What is actually true"; (3) a verified assessment after the low-cost fixes in the action plan.
- **Assumption:** where a profile records no claim by the owner about a control, the self-assessment uses the same level as the evidence. That favours self-assessment, so the real difference between what owners believe and what is true is probably larger than shown.
- **Assurance gap:** a control the owner rates 2+ that the evidence shows is 1 or less.
- **Planted gap "found":** any control mapped to that gap in `data/answer-keys.json` scores 1 or less in the round. **"Closed":** the gap's primary (first-listed) control reaches level 2.

**Limitation, stated plainly:** the same author wrote the profiles, the answer keys and the scored rounds, so "the verified round finds the planted gaps" is close to circular and is shown for completeness, not as proof. The two results that do test something are the **coverage check** (does the question bank's evidence step reach every planted gap?) and the **self-assessment comparison** (how much would asking the owner alone have missed?).

## Coverage check of the question bank

`node tools/coverage.mjs` checks that every planted gap's evidence appears in the "how to check" step of one of its controls.

- **First run: 37 of 40 covered.** Uncovered: the clinic's missing process for patients asking for their records; the tuition centre's reused class links; tutors messaging students with no rules.
- **Fixes:** PR-13 now asks how access and correction requests are handled (21 days); PR-4 now covers online meeting rooms and waiting rooms; GV-3 now checks for rules on contacting customers, students or patients from personal phones (and the acceptable use policy gained that section).
- **Second run: 40 of 40 covered.**

**Across all five:** asking the owner alone would have found **23 of 40** planted gaps (57.5%); checking the evidence found **40 of 40**. There were **37 assurance gaps**, controls the owner believed were in place but weren't. The low-cost fixes cost **RM3,850 one-off and RM370 a month in total** (about 112 hours of work across the five), and leave **3 of 40** planted gaps open.

## What this shows

1. **Self-assessment overstates security.** Every business rated itself higher than the evidence. A checklist that only asks "do you have backups?" would have passed most of these businesses.
2. **The worst gaps are the ones owners are sure about** ("Google keeps everything backed up", "each staff member has their own login"). The assessment's "how to check" step and the inventory hints are what catch them.
3. **Most fixes are free.** MFA, removing old accounts, changing default passwords, separating guest Wi-Fi, notices and consent, policies and a breach plan cost little or nothing. The expensive items (replacing an unsupported server, managed laptops) can be planned and budgeted.
4. **Residual gaps are expected and visible.** "After" is not 100%; levels 2 → 3 need regular checking over time, and some fixes are scheduled for 2027.

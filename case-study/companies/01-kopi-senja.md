# 01 Kopi Senja (fictional)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Cafe, breakfast and evening drinks |
| Location | Ipoh, Perak (one outlet) |
| Staff | 6 (owner + 5 part-time and full-time staff) |
| Size | Micro enterprise |
| Owner | Hafiz, 34, ex-barista. Comfortable with phones, not with "IT". |
| Security budget | About RM100 a month, maybe a one-off RM1,000 |

## How the business runs
- Cloud POS on an Android tablet at the counter, with a bank card terminal. No card numbers are stored by the cafe.
- Orders also come through two food-delivery apps, each running on its own phone.
- A WhatsApp Business account for orders and promos. A **broadcast list of about 800 customer numbers** collected at the counter for a "10th drink free" loyalty card.
- 4 CCTV cameras recording to a DVR in the store room, viewable on the owner's phone app.
- One router from the ISP: the same Wi-Fi network for customers, the POS tablet and the CCTV.
- One shared laptop in the back office for supplier invoices, staff rota (Google Sheets) and Canva.
- Staff records (IC copies, bank account numbers for salary) as photos in the owner's phone gallery and a Google Drive folder.

## What the owner says
- "We don't really have data, we're just a cafe."
- "The Wi-Fi has a password, it's on the menu board."
- "Only I know the POS admin password."
- "Google keeps everything backed up."

## What is actually true (planted gaps)
1. Guest Wi-Fi, POS and CCTV share **one flat network**, and the router admin login is still the default printed on the sticker.
2. The DVR has the **default admin password** and has remote viewing turned on through the internet.
3. The POS admin PIN is written on a sticky note under the counter. Two former staff still know it and their POS logins were never removed.
4. The loyalty list was collected with **no notice** about how numbers will be used, and promos go to people who never agreed to marketing.
5. Staff IC and bank photos sit in the owner's phone gallery, which syncs to a personal cloud account with no 2-step verification.
6. The shared laptop has one Windows account with no password, and the Google account on it stays logged in.
7. No CCTV signage about recording, no retention period (the DVR keeps about 30 days and overwrites).
8. Nothing is backed up outside Google: if the Google account is taken over, the rota, invoices and staff records go with it.

## Incident history
- Last year the cafe's Instagram account was briefly taken over after the owner entered his password on a fake "copyright violation" page. He got it back after a week.

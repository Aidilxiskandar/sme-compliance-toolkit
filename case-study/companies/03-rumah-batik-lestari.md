# 03 Rumah Batik Lestari (fictional)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | Online shop selling handmade batik and modest wear |
| Location | Kota Bharu, Kelantan (home workshop + small warehouse) |
| Staff | 4 (two sister founders, 1 packer, 1 freelance social media admin) |
| Size | Micro enterprise |
| Owners | Aisyah, 29 (marketing and live selling) and Nurul, 31 (operations and finance) |
| Security budget | About RM150 a month |

## How the business runs
- Sells on two marketplace platforms, a social-commerce shop with **live-stream selling**, and its own website on a hosted e-commerce platform.
- Payments go through the platforms and a payment gateway. The shop never sees card numbers, but it does get names, addresses and phone numbers for every order.
- Runs paid ads from a social media business account. The ad account has a company credit card attached.
- A freelance admin (works remotely, not an employee) manages the social accounts and replies to DMs.
- Order lists are **exported to spreadsheets** for packing and courier booking, then printed.
- Two laptops (one per founder) and their phones. Banking on the business account through mobile banking.

## Personal data held
- About 15,000 past customers: names, delivery addresses, phone numbers, order history.
- Customer sizes and photos sent in DMs for custom tailoring.
- The freelancer's and packer's IC and bank details.

## What the owners say
- "The platforms handle security, not us."
- "Our accounts have strong passwords."
- "We only share access with people we trust."

## What is actually true (planted gaps)
1. The founders and the freelancer **share one login** for the social media accounts and the marketplace seller centre, and the password has been reused on other sites.
2. 2-step verification is on for the marketplaces but **off on the social media account** that owns the ad account and the credit card.
3. The freelancer still has full admin rights from the previous freelancer, who left a year ago and was **never removed**.
4. Hundreds of order export spreadsheets with customer addresses sit in the laptops' Downloads folders and in a shared cloud folder with an **"anyone with the link" share**.
5. Printed packing lists with names, addresses and phone numbers go in the normal rubbish bin.
6. The website platform admin account belongs to Aisyah's personal email; if she loses it, the business loses the shop.
7. The privacy notice on the website was copied from another shop and names a different business.
8. No agreement with the freelancer about protecting customer data (under the PDPA amendments, data processors have their own security duties).

## Incident history
- Customers have reported getting WhatsApp messages from someone pretending to be the shop, asking for payment to "release a parcel stuck at customs". The owners don't know how the scammers got the order details.
